# v0.1 authored-project smoke results

This is plumbing evidence, not a comparative benchmark claim. It uses one search seed, one runtime seed, and a budget of 100 transitions. The purpose is to prove that the pinned projects compile/load, all strategies share the same budgeted controller, reports remain tier-separated, and paired coverage evidence is reproducible.

Configuration:

- corpus: `authored-corpus-v1`;
- stories: Dog Ink Adventure, The Intercept, Heresy II;
- algorithms: random, systematic, coverage, minimal swarm;
- search seed: 1;
- story seed: 1;
- budget: 100 choice transitions;
- runtime: `inkjs` 2.4.0;
- compiler artifact: official `inklecate` 1.2.1 with `-c`;
- command: `npm run corpus:smoke`.

All 12 cells completed and none recorded a runtime warning or error. Empirical counts were:

| Dog Ink Adventure | systematic | 4 | 5 | 101 |
| Dog Ink Adventure | coverage | 5 | 20 | 100 |
| Dog Ink Adventure | swarm | 5 | 19 | 75 |

| The Intercept | systematic | 21 | 73 | 73 |

At this one paired seed, the strategies produced exclusive edge evidence: random 10 and swarm 1 on Dog Ink Adventure; random 4, systematic 31, coverage 37, and swarm 10 on The Intercept; random 2 and coverage 5 on Heresy II. These are examples of complementarity, not estimates of expected performance. Repeated paired seeds and larger preregistered budgets are required before drawing conclusions.

The real InkCheck 0.7.2 adapter also completed each story at a 100-state native budget. InkCheck state counts are not equated with in-process choice transitions, and its unavailable item-level coverage remains `null`.

## Compiler compatibility finding

Heresy II is valid under official `inklecate` 1.2.1 but triggers `Unexpected object type in NumberExpression` in the `inkjs` 2.4.0 compiler because of floating-point expressions. InkBench does not rewrite or drop the story. The authored tier loads digest-pinned official compiler output into the same `inkjs` runtime for every in-process strategy, while the InkCheck adapter compiles the pinned sources through InkCheck's official compiler path. This keeps compiler compatibility from being misreported as search performance.
